I want you to conduct a comprehensive visual design audit of BaseLodge and create a complete screenshot export that documents the current experience.

For this exercise, act as if you are the lead mobile product designer responsible for digital experiences at brands such as Aman Hotels, Four Seasons, and A. Lange & Söhne.

I am not asking you to copy those brands or imitate their visual identities. Use them as the standard for the level of taste, restraint, attention to detail, craftsmanship, consistency, and premium experience I want you to bring to the critique.

BaseLodge should still feel like BaseLodge. The objective is to identify what is preventing the existing product from feeling exceptionally polished and premium, and what we could realistically do to improve it.

## PART 1: DOCUMENT THE CURRENT EXPERIENCE

Do not change any application code, styling, behavior, database content, deployment configuration, or production settings.

Programmatically visit all major user-facing BaseLodge routes and screens that are currently accessible.

Use the existing app environment and available test/dev data.

Capture the most representative populated state for each screen.

Where materially different, also capture important alternate states such as:

* empty states
* logged-out states
* tabs
* modal/open states
* public vs. private states
* filled vs. unfilled profile states
* loading or transitional states if visually important
* any other state that creates a meaningfully different visual experience

Capture both:

* desktop
* mobile

Because BaseLodge is a mobile app, give particular attention to how the experience works and feels at mobile dimensions.

Use full-page screenshots where appropriate.

Do not omit a screen simply because it looks visually similar to another screen if it represents a distinct part of the BaseLodge user experience.

## SCREENSHOT FILENAMES

Every screenshot filename must clearly explain what is shown.

Use this naming convention:

[screen-or-feature]*[specific-state-or-tab]*[viewport].png

Examples:

* home_logged-in_desktop.png
* home_empty-state_mobile.png
* mountain-vail_overview_desktop.png
* mountain-vail_friends-tab_mobile.png
* trip-detail_public_desktop.png
* trip-detail_private_mobile.png
* profile_gear-filled_desktop.png
* profile_gear-empty_mobile.png
* friends_list_desktop.png
* notifications_empty_mobile.png

Do not use generic filenames such as:

* screenshot-1.png
* page-2.png
* image.png
* route-3.png

If a screenshot represents a specific tab, modal, empty state, logged-out state, public/private state, or other meaningful variation, that distinction must be included in the filename.

Someone should be able to understand what each screenshot represents from the filename alone.

## SCREENSHOT INDEX

Organize the screenshots into logical folders by product area or feature where helpful.

Also create an index that lists, for every screenshot:

* filename
* route or URL path
* desktop or mobile
* state captured
* brief description of what is shown

Package the complete screenshot set and index into a single downloadable ZIP file.

## PART 2: CONDUCT A PREMIUM DESIGN AUDIT

After documenting the current experience, review BaseLodge as a premium mobile product designer.

Approach the critique with the level of scrutiny you would use if you were reviewing a digital product for Aman Hotels, Four Seasons, or A. Lange & Söhne.

Do not simply tell me that something "could be more premium."

Identify specifically:

1. What currently feels wrong, weak, generic, inconsistent, unfinished, cluttered, dated, utilitarian, or visually inexpensive.

2. Why it creates that impression.

3. What a more sophisticated treatment would accomplish.

4. Specifically how you would improve it.

Evaluate areas including, but not limited to:

* typography
* typography hierarchy
* spacing
* visual rhythm
* information density
* whitespace
* color usage
* contrast
* cards and containers
* borders
* shadows
* corner radii
* buttons and CTAs
* navigation
* icons
* imagery
* avatars
* forms
* tabs
* pills and badges
* modals
* empty states
* loading states
* data presentation
* mountain information
* trip presentation
* social/friend information
* hierarchy between primary and secondary information
* consistency across screens
* transitions and micro-interactions where relevant
* overall visual identity

Pay particular attention to small details. Premium products often feel premium because dozens of small decisions are handled exceptionally well rather than because of one dramatic redesign.

## DESIGN PHILOSOPHY

Use Aman, Four Seasons, and A. Lange & Söhne as references for principles such as:

* restraint
* confidence
* craftsmanship
* intentionality
* excellent typography
* disciplined use of space
* strong hierarchy
* consistency
* subtle details
* high-quality imagery
* avoiding unnecessary visual noise
* making important information feel effortless to understand

But do not turn BaseLodge into a hotel website or luxury watch website.

BaseLodge is still a social ski and mountain travel product.

It should retain personality, warmth, energy, usefulness, and a sense of community.

The goal is not "luxury" for the sake of luxury.

The goal is for BaseLodge to feel exceptionally well designed, considered, modern, and premium.

## THINK SYSTEMICALLY

Do not give me a collection of isolated cosmetic suggestions.

Look for underlying design-system problems.

For example, if ten screens feel crowded because spacing is inconsistent, identify the spacing-system problem rather than giving me ten separate recommendations to add padding.

If cards throughout the app feel generic, identify what is causing the BaseLodge card system to feel generic and propose a better system.

If hierarchy is weak throughout the app, explain what needs to change globally.

Separate your recommendations into:

### A. App-wide design system improvements

Changes that would improve BaseLodge across the entire product.

### B. Reusable component improvements

Changes to recurring components such as cards, buttons, navigation, avatars, tabs, pills, forms, mountain modules, trip modules, etc.

### C. Screen-specific improvements

Problems or opportunities that genuinely belong to an individual screen or experience.

## PRIORITIZATION

For every meaningful recommendation, indicate:

* impact on perceived quality: Low / Medium / High
* implementation effort: Low / Medium / High
* whether it appears to be a global/system change or screen-specific change

Also identify:

### Quick wins

Relatively small changes that could materially improve perceived quality.

### Foundational improvements

Changes to the underlying design system that would make many screens better at once.

### Larger opportunities

More substantial redesigns or experience changes that may be worth considering later.

## IMPORTANT CONSTRAINT

This is an audit and recommendation exercise only.

Do not implement any changes.

Do not modify BaseLodge.

Do not create commits.

Do not alter production or development data.

Do not start redesigning screens.

I want to understand what is wrong, what could be better, why it matters, and what you recommend doing before deciding what we actually implement.

Before beginning the audit, first provide me with the proposed inventory of routes, screens, and states you intend to capture so I can review whether the coverage is complete.
