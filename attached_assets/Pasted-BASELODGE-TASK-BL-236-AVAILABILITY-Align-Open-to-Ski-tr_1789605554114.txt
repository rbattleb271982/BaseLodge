PRODUCT MODEL

Availability is canonical.

When a user adds Availability, they are saying:

- they are available
- they want to ski or snowboard
- they are open to making a plan

"Open to Ski" is the first-person social presentation of canonical Availability.

It is NOT:
- a second data model
- a separate persistence state
- a Trip
- a commitment

LOCKED TERMINOLOGY

Use "Availability" for setup and management language.

Examples:
- Add availability
- Edit availability
- Your availability

Use "Open to Ski" when presenting the user's availability socially in first person.

When referring to another person, use normal availability language.

Example:
"Richard is available Jan 15–18"

Do not awkwardly describe another user as "Open to Ski."

MOUNTAIN SEMANTICS

A mountain attached to Availability means:

"The user is interested in skiing this mountain during these dates."

It does NOT mean:
- they have a Trip there
- they have committed to the destination
- they are definitely going there

Availability without a mountain means the destination is undecided.

DATE-FIRST MATCHING

Dates are the primary matching signal.

Two eligible friends may have a meaningful Availability overlap even if:

- their mountain interests differ
- only one selected a mountain
- neither selected a mountain

Do not require matching mountain IDs for a date overlap to exist.

Preserve existing reciprocal-friend/privacy eligibility rules.

TRIPS REMAIN SEPARATE

Do not merge Trips and Availability.

Do not make Trips own Availability.

Do not automatically synchronize them.

However:

When a user CREATES or JOINS a Trip whose dates overlap their existing canonical Availability, ask whether they want to remove the overlapping dates from Availability.

This must be an explicit user choice.

NEVER automatically remove Availability.

If they choose KEEP:
- make no Availability mutation
- existing canonical Availability remains unchanged
- matching continues to use those dates

If they choose REMOVE:
- remove only the Availability dates overlapping the Trip
- preserve any non-overlapping Availability
- subsequent matching must reflect the canonical change

Example:

Availability:
Jan 10–20

Trip:
Jan 15–18

If user removes the overlap, preserve:
Jan 10–14
Jan 19–20

Do not delete the entire Availability range merely because part overlaps.

Investigate existing UserAvailability representation before implementing this. Handle splitting/range behavior consistently with the current data model and transaction patterns.

If multiple Availability ranges overlap the Trip, handle all relevant overlapping portions correctly.

The prompt should happen only after the Trip create/join action has actually succeeded.

Do not optimistically mutate Availability based on an unconfirmed Trip action.

PRIVACY EXPLANATION

The Availability editor should clearly explain the existing privacy model.

The user should understand:

- their raw Availability is private
- BaseLodge does not expose their complete Availability calendar to friends
- eligible friends may see derived overlap/context when their dates intersect

Keep this explanation concise and calm.

Do not introduce alarming privacy language.

Do not change the privacy architecture.

DATE PRESENTATION

Audit the current Availability/Open to Ski surfaces and standardize date formatting.

The underlying convention should be consistent while allowing layout-specific presentation where necessary.

Avoid situations where the same Availability appears with unnecessarily different date semantics across:

- Availability management
- Home/Open to Ski
- friend/social overlap
- Mountain Detail overlap
- Trip-overlap prompt
- other current contextual Availability surfaces

Reuse an existing shared formatter if one exists.

If formatting logic is currently duplicated, consolidate only where doing so is bounded and safe.

Do not turn this into a broad date/time architecture rewrite.

EXPERIENCE INTENT

For social overlap, the user should immediately understand:

1. WHO they overlap with
2. WHEN the overlap exists
3. WHY it is relevant

The feeling should be:

"Hey, we could actually ski together."

Not:

"We are expected to make a Trip."

Overlap represents POSSIBILITY, not commitment.

Do not introduce:

- countdowns
- urgency
- scarcity
- FOMO language
- pressure to create a Trip
- gamification
- engagement mechanics

The natural next action may be exploring the overlap or making a plan, but action must remain optional.

DENSE STATES

Validate behavior for mature accounts, including approximately:

- 25+ friends
- 15+ Trips
- multiple Availability ranges
- several simultaneous overlaps
- differing mountain interests
- years of accumulated activity/history

Do not solve dense states by exposing raw Availability.

Do not assume only one overlap exists.
