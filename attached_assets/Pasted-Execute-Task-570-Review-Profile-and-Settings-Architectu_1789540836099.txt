#570 — Review Profile and Settings Architecture

## Objective

Perform an evidence-based product architecture review of BaseLodge's current Profile, Settings, and related edit/manage destinations.

Establish:

1. What Profile currently owns and functions as.
2. What Settings currently owns and functions as.
3. Where personal information is displayed versus edited.
4. Where users must go to manage information they see elsewhere.
5. Which repetitions are useful contextual repetition.
6. Which are inconsistent duplication, redundant workflows, ownership mismatches, or legacy/presentation debt.
7. Whether any standalone screens/routes may no longer justify their existence.
8. What coherent future architectures are possible.

Do NOT select the future architecture. The product owner will make those decisions after reviewing your findings.

## Locked Availability direction

Do not reopen the Availability architecture decision.

The following decisions are already locked:

- Availability is the canonical functional concept.
- “Add availability” is appropriate management language.
- Open to Ski is a first-person presentation of canonical Availability, not a separate data model.
- Trips remain separate from Availability.
- Raw Availability remains private.
- Friends may receive eligible derived overlap information, not the user's raw private Availability calendar.
- A mountain attached to Availability represents interest, not a committed Trip.
- Availability without a mountain means destination undecided.
- Availability matching is date-first.
- Profile placement of Availability has NOT yet been decided.

For this review, determine how Availability currently intersects Profile and Settings and what placement possibilities each future architecture creates. Do not choose its future location.

## 6. Classify duplication

Every meaningful repeated concept should be classified as one of:

### A. Useful contextual repetition
The same canonical information appropriately appears in multiple contexts.

### B. Inconsistent duplication
The same concept appears differently or communicates conflicting semantics.

### C. Redundant workflow
Multiple places effectively perform the same management job without a clear product reason.

### D. Ownership mismatch
Information appears to belong to one product surface but must be managed somewhere conceptually unrelated.

### E. Legacy/presentation debt
A screen, route, control, hierarchy, or workflow appears to survive primarily because of historical implementation rather than current product architecture.

Do not call something redundant merely because the same canonical data is shown in multiple places.
