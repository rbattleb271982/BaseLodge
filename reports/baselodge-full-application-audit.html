BaseLodge Full Application Audit

Product, Performance, UX, Privacy, Accessibility & Architecture

Audit type: Evidence-based audit only; no application fixes implemented.

1. Executive summary

BaseLodge's core social trip-planning architecture is substantially mature. Privacy boundaries, authorization rules, lifecycle handling, responsive layouts, and state transitions have broad automated coverage.

  • No P0 or P1 defects
  • Five material P2 defects plus several P3 maintenance and interaction defects
  • No confirmed authorization or private-data leakage defect
  • No confirmed N+1 or severe server-performance regression
  • Payload hotspots on Trip Detail, Friends, and Home
  • Five stale Python assertions and two broken visual-capture states
  • Major information-architecture opportunities around Profile/Settings, availability, invitations, and notifications

The most important confirmed user-facing defect is malformed equipment IDs causing generic 500 errors. The most important reliability concern is Notifications silently presenting database failures as an empty inbox.

8. Confirmed bugs

D1 — Malformed equipment IDs produce 500 responses

Classification: Reliability · Confidence: Confirmed · Severity: P2

Authenticated probes sent setup_id=abc to save, make-primary and delete. All returned generic 500 pages. Direct int(setup_id) calls need guarded parsing and stable 400 responses. No migration required.

D2 — Notification query failures look like an empty inbox

Classification: Reliability/UX · Confidence: High Confidence · Severity: P2

Pending-request and activity queries catch broad exceptions, roll back, then render normal empty collections. A database failure can hide social activity without telling the user. Failures need privacy-safe logging and an explicit degraded state.

D3 — Authentication mode controls are inaccessible

Classification: Accessibility · Confidence: High Confidence · Severity: P2

The login/create-account switch uses clickable divs without keyboard focusability, tab/button roles, selected state or panel association.

D4 — Login errors are not announced

Classification: Accessibility · Confidence: High Confidence · Severity: P2

The dynamic error lacks an alert/live-region role, field association and focus handling.

D5 — Notification actions have indistinguishable pending and failed states

Classification: UX/Reliability · Confidence: High Confidence · Severity: P2

Rows dim during requests, but controls do not expose busy state and failure silently restores the row.

D6 — Shared focus styling can hide keyboard focus

Classification: Accessibility · Confidence: High Confidence · Severity: P2

Shared CSS removes form-control outlines and relies on subtle border changes. A consistent high-visibility :focus-visible treatment is needed.

D7 — Two capture states cannot complete

Classification: Maintenance · Confidence: Confirmed · Severity: P3

Home availability targets a hidden trigger; Trip Detail invite-modal activates an overlay that remains non-visible.

D8 — Five Python assertions are stale

Classification: Maintenance · Confidence: Confirmed · Severity: P3

Four assertions expect removed/renamed Trip Detail presentation; one expects the former nested Friends' Trips destination shape.

9. Performance findings

No severe performance defect was confirmed. Priorities are Trip Detail's 195 KB document, Friends' 139 KB payload, Home's critical-path aggregation, Mountains' full-catalog data architecture, targeted refresh POST+GET behavior, and unknown intent-prefetch waste.

10. Loading-state findings

Mountain social loading, Friends paging and targeted refresh have strong deduplication, retry and stale-response protections. Gaps remain in notification action feedback, shared navigation progress cancellation, form-submission loading, and production measurement of Home's external/degraded paths.

11. Responsive findings

No global horizontal overflow was observed. Concerns remain around controls below the app's 48–52px interaction sizing, clipped long metadata/settings values, desktop layouts that mainly stretch mobile UI, and multiple fixed/sticky interaction systems on Trip Detail.

12. Accessibility findings

  • Non-semantic authentication tabs
  • Unannounced login errors
  • Suppressed focus outlines
  • Incomplete dialog contracts across some sheets
  • Undersized avatar, chip, filter and text-link controls
  • No busy/failure semantics for notification mutations
  • Inconsistent arrow and back-control labeling

Strong existing behavior includes availability-sheet focus trapping/restoration, Friends paging focus preservation and stale-response safeguards.

13. Privacy/authorization findings

No confirmed authorization or private-data leakage defect was found. Existing coverage includes private trips, reciprocal-friend requirements, invitation isolation, organizer/participant controls, planning visibility, availability privacy, equipment ownership, terminal-trip exclusion, CSRF and log privacy.

The notification-viewed endpoint is protected by the global unsafe-method CSRF hook; a raw authenticated tokenless POST returned 403.

14. State-consistency findings

No new cross-screen state inconsistency was reproduced. Positive evidence includes latest-response-only refresh, duplicate-submit prevention, monotonic paging generations, invite invalidation, canonical lifecycle filtering and equipment-primary normalization. Participant-specific effective-date coverage should be rewritten against the current Friends' Trips shape.

15. Empty/error/edge-state findings

The matrix covered empty friends/trips/wishlist/notifications, no-results discovery, no dates, validation errors, long/dense states, system errors and terminal trips. Material gaps are notification outage ambiguity, malformed equipment 500s, silent notification action failure and the two obsolete capture states.

16. Console/network findings

No unexpected console errors occurred on normal completed captures. Intentional 403/404/500 states produced expected errors. Favicon requests were occasionally browser-aborted although the asset returned 200 directly. Google Fonts failed only under deliberately blocked external networking and retained system fallbacks. Malformed equipment probes produced the three reproducible 500s.

17. Code-path findings

  • Broad exception swallowing in Notifications
  • Unguarded integer parsing in equipment handlers
  • Obsolete Trip Detail refresh/presentation assumptions
  • Multiple critical-path modules in Home
  • Full client-side Mountains catalog
  • No new unsafe GET mutation confirmed

18. Navigation/information-architecture findings

  1. Documented and shipped primary navigation disagree
  2. Profile and Settings lack one clear canonical model
  3. Availability is fragmented among editing, Open to Ski, overlap, Ideas and trip creation
  4. Invitation behavior is split among friend, trip, group and share-link flows
  5. Notifications is useful but buried
  6. Legacy aliases obscure canonical destinations

19. Design-system consistency findings

The app has a recognizable visual language but multiple button families, sheet/modal implementations, focus/loading/disabled behaviors, headers, arrows, tabs, empty states and destructive-action patterns. Highest-value standards: one dialog primitive, semantic button variants, a focus policy, one tab/disclosure contract, one async state contract and one header/back family.

20. Terminology/copy mismatches

Mountain/resort, wishlist/bucket list, friend/connection, invite/join request, availability/Open to Ski, planning/Ideas, Profile/Settings and Create Trip/Add Trip need consistent product definitions while preserving real differences among social and participant states.

21. Feature-discoverability findings

Availability is distributed across Home, Profile, Ideas and standalone routes. Equipment is mostly account configuration rather than trip context. Notifications lacks a persistent entry. Planning depends on contextual discovery. Ski history and visited mountains are weakly connected to planning. Suggested Friends competes with many responsibilities inside Friends.

22. Cross-screen duplication findings

Useful: resort identity, trip status and friend context in relevant surfaces. Inconsistent: wishlist terminology, friend terminology, availability models and invite mechanisms. Duplicate implementation: modals, sheets, buttons, back controls and async state handling.

34. Overall BaseLodge product assessment

Strongest: social-trip authorization, lifecycle modeling, Home hierarchy, stale-response protection, social privacy, fixture coverage and mobile-first visual language.

Weakest: authentication accessibility, notification failure semantics, accumulated modal/sheet implementations, Profile/Settings architecture, availability and invitation fragmentation, stale regression contracts and absent route budgets.

The app has outgrown its earlier screen-oriented architecture. Home is now a coordination center; Mountains is social discovery; Profile includes account/history/gear/preferences; and Availability spans several planning flows.

35. Five highest-value areas for product-owner review

  1. Availability as one planning workflow
  2. Canonical Profile/Settings architecture
  3. Pending actions and Notifications
  4. Accessible shared interaction system
  5. Trip Detail and Friends payload architecture

36. Exact recommended next action

Approve a first implementation batch for controlled equipment-ID validation, explicit Notification failure/retry states, accessible authentication switching/error announcement, and repair of stale tests and capture states. Then conduct a separate product-design plan for unified Availability and canonical Profile/Settings architecture.
