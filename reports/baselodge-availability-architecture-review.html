1. Current Availability architecture

BaseLodge currently has four distinct layers:

  1. Canonical Availability: a user’s private, destination-neutral future ski dates, managed through /add-open-dates.
  2. Derived Availability: intersections with friends, friend trips, a specific trip, mountains, Ideas, and activities.
  3. Trip-specific dates: trip windows, participant attendance dates, RSVP, and invitation state. These remain independent.
  4. Open to Ski: an owner-selected static sharing snapshot built from canonical Availability, with no second live availability store.

High-level flow: UserAvailability → shared resolver → Home, Ideas, overlaps, Trip Detail, Mountain Detail, activities, and Open to Ski. The architecture is more unified in data than it appears in terminology and presentation.

3. Canonical data/source-of-truth analysis

Canonical data

UserAvailability stores one normalized record per user/calendar date with a unique user/date constraint. Database values are SQL/Python dates; service and presentation boundaries use strict ISO YYYY-MM-DD. Past dates are excluded from current resolution.

Legacy compatibility

User.open_dates remains a JSON compatibility mirror and fallback. A normalized decision wins per date; a false normalized row suppresses a legacy true value. Full replacement updates normalized rows and mirrors sorted ISO dates.

Mutations and reads

  • Create/edit: one POST to the calendar replaces the complete future selection.
  • Delete: omit dates from replacement or delete all rows during account deletion.
  • Reads: shared single/batch resolvers support editor, Home, Ideas, social comparisons, Trip/Mountain Detail, Open to Ski, and activity generation.

Classification

  • Canonical: normalized Availability rows.
  • Derived: overlaps, Ideas, trip projections, mountain summaries, activities.
  • Presentation-only: Open to Ski’s selected subset and review fingerprint.
  • Trip-specific: trip and participant dates, RSVP, invitations, planning items.

4. Availability relationship to Trips

QuestionCurrent answer
Does creating a trip affect Availability?No. It can trigger derived overlap recomputation.
Does joining a trip affect Availability?No. Participation and attendance are separate.
Do trip dates become Availability?No.
Does Availability constrain trip dates?No.
Are attendance dates independent?Yes—from both the trip window and canonical Availability.
Can Availability conflict with a trip?Yes; current semantics allow it.

Trip Detail intersects the viewer’s Availability with the trip calendar and shows all/some/no available days. Availability is an opportunity signal, not an RSVP, booking, attendance record, or resort commitment.

5. Social/friend-overlap architecture

Social calculations require reciprocal current friendship. Raw friend calendars are not generally exposed. BaseLodge derives limited coordination information such as shared dates, overlap ranges, a viewer’s Availability intersecting a friend trip, and mountain-relevant summaries.

  • Direct friend overlap does not require a trip.
  • Friend-trip overlap intersects viewer Availability with eligible future friend trips.
  • Trip Detail exposes the viewer’s own projection, not another user’s full calendar.
  • Ideas convert availability matches into planning candidates.
  • Availability changes rebuild relevant derived activity evidence.

The privacy boundary is sound. However, “Only you can see your availability” should distinguish private raw dates from limited derived overlap shown to eligible friends.

6. Open to Ski relationship

Open to Ski is an owner-controlled sharing workflow built from canonical future Availability. The user reviews dates, chooses a subset, generates a 4:5 preview, and shares or downloads the image.

It is not a second Availability store, live public calendar, trip, invitation, RSVP, friend request, resort commitment, recipient-addressed message, or automatically updating link.

The workflow fingerprints the reviewed canonical state and rejects a stale preview if source dates change. Shared image copies remain static. Its distinct value is cross-channel social expression; its limitation is having no recipient, response, chosen destination, or in-app planning state.

Conclusion: Open to Ski has a distinct sharing job, but not a distinct Availability data concept. Whether that job deserves separate product naming is a product-owner decision.

7. Current user journeys

No Availability

Home and Ideas prompt the user to add dates; the calendar is empty; Profile can show a transient post-pass nudge; Open to Ski has a no-dates state; Trip Detail reports no Availability for its window.

Availability but no trips

Home summary, direct friend overlap, Ideas, recommendations, friend context, and Open to Ski can provide value without a trip.

Overlapping friends

Matches can appear through Home/Ideas, idea detail, Friend Profile, combined Trips opportunities, Mountain context, and derived activity evidence. Full private calendars remain hidden.

Availability and an existing trip

Trip Detail reports all/some/no available trip days. Runtime verification confirmed that April Availability did not make a January trip appear available.

Changing Availability

Saving replaces the complete future set, updates the legacy mirror, recomputes overlap evidence, and redirects to Home. Calendar, Home, Open to Ski, Trip projections, and Ideas reflect changes after normal navigation/reload. Open to Ski detects stale review state explicitly.

9. Missing connections

  1. Profile lacks a persistent Availability status/management entry.
  2. Trip creation does not offer recorded Availability as an optional date starting point or explain independence.
  3. Joining a trip does not reconcile Availability; this may be intentional but is unexplained.
  4. Mountain overlap lacks a consistently direct plan-from-overlap action.
  5. Open to Ski has no in-app recipient, date response, resort suggestion, or conversion path.
  6. Availability overlap activities and Ideas may represent overlapping user jobs.
  7. Trip Detail does not explain attendance versus canonical Availability.

10. Standalone Availability destination assessment

The dedicated editor uniquely supports destination-neutral multi-month management, disjoint ranges, clearing, full-set replacement, privacy explanation, and sharing access.

  • Profile: natural persistent summary/link, but embedding the full calendar could overload Profile.
  • Home: natural contextual entry because Availability drives Ideas, but full editing would compete with coordination content.
  • Trips/planning: important consumers, but cannot fully own Availability because it exists before a trip.

The evidence supports retaining a dedicated edit surface even if Availability is not treated as a primary navigation destination or separate product brand.

12. Design/presentation consistency assessment

Canonical management pattern: the multi-month editor, with clear ranges, removal, clearing, empty guidance, privacy explanation, and one save action.

Canonical contextual summary: Home’s Availability sheet, combining current ranges, purpose, and edit action.

Canonical trip projection: Trip Detail’s day strip and available-day count.

Justified variant: Open to Ski’s editorial share-card design. Its visual distinction fits external sharing, but the transition from private source to static share needs clearer explanation.

Current inconsistencies include range formatting, Add/Edit terminology, “Open Dates” browser title, empty-state explanations, hierarchy, and selection semantics.

14. Future-state product architecture options

No option is selected.

Option A — Preserve current architecture with targeted clarification

Concept: Keep current state boundaries and treat the problem as terminology, explanation, and presentation drift.

Management: Dedicated calendar. Open to Ski: static card. Home/Profile: current Home behavior; minimal Profile change. Trips/Ideas/Mountains: current projections remain.

Changes: standard terminology, privacy explanation, attendance explanation, shared range formatting. Disappears: most synonym drift.

Benefit: Lowest risk. Tradeoff: Distributed journey and static share dead-end remain. Complexity: Low. Migration: No migration likely.

Option B — One canonical Availability system with contextual presentation

Concept: Availability becomes the single explicit concept. Open to Ski becomes the “Share availability” action/artifact.

Management: Dedicated calendar, persistently reachable from Home and Profile. Home: summary and Ideas. Trips: independent with clearer explanation. Ideas/Mountains: clearer planning handoffs.

Stays: all core capabilities and static card. Changes: vocabulary, persistent Profile access, shared formatting, privacy explanation, contextual transitions. Disappears: expectation that Open to Ski is separate state.

Benefit: Understandable and discoverable without losing capability. Tradeoff: coordinated cross-surface work; Profile density. Complexity: Medium. Migration: No migration likely.

Option C — Live social Open to Ski

Concept: Open to Ski becomes a persisted, scoped share with expiry, response, current/stale state, and planning handoff.

Management: Existing calendar remains source. Open to Ski: live social object; static image optional. Home/Profile: share status and responses. Trips/Ideas: responses can become stronger planning signals. Mountains: optional destination during planning.

Benefit: Converts expression into measurable coordination. Tradeoff: privacy, permissions, stale links, recipients, notifications, lifecycle, and overlap with invites/Ideas. Complexity: High. Migration: Migration may be required for a share/response entity.

Option D — Unify into Ideas/planning and retire Open to Ski

Concept: Keep private Availability but make Ideas, overlap, Mountain context, and trip planning its only social expression.

Management: Dedicated calendar. Open to Ski: removed/deprecated. Home: summary and Ideas become primary. Trips/Mountains: direct contextual planning.

Stays: canonical data, editor, overlaps, Ideas, trip/mountain projections. Disappears: share preview, export, route, analytics, and possibly duplicate overlap activities.

Benefit: Fewest concepts and direct in-app planning. Tradeoff: Loses lightweight cross-channel social expression. Complexity: Medium technically, high product significance. Migration: No data migration likely; deprecation required.

15. Migration implications

  • Current architecture: no migration required.
  • Removing legacy open_dates: separate migration and complete reader audit required.
  • Option A: no migration likely.
  • Option B: no migration likely.
  • Option C: migration may be required for share scope, expiry, responses, status, and optional destination.
  • Option D: no data migration likely; route, analytics, and client deprecation work remains.

Any automatic trip/attendance synchronization would require provenance, conflict, override, and historical rules; it is not a simple presentation change.
